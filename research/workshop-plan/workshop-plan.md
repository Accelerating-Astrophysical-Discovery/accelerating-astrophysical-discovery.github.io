The workshop is designed as an open-source project hackathon. The goal is to create shared understanding, build momentum, and leave with living assets rather than only discussion notes.

Participants will self-organise into four KPA teams plus an implementation team. Each KPA team defines scientific use cases, data requirements, tokenisation semantics, and research uses. The implementation team identifies the technical stack, coordinates open-source development, and turns the week into an actionable MVP plan.

## Monday: scientific problems and shared aims

The first day asks: who are we, and what scientific problems are we uniting to solve? The workshop opens with the KPAs, scientific possibilities, and team structure. Participants refine the KPAs, self-organise into teams, and develop ideal-world end-user use cases that go beyond next-token prediction into practical scientific workflows.

In the afternoon, KPA teams map the challenges of those use cases after a framing talk on building, training, and deploying foundation models. The implementation team surveys skills and records gaps. The day closes with KPA challenge pitches and a shared list of missing capabilities.

## Tuesday: data, tokenisation, and relevancy

Tuesday asks what data must flow into the system and how it should be represented. KPA teams identify relevant datasets, contrast proposed workflows with traditional analysis, assess accessibility, and search for existing examples.

The afternoon focuses on tokenisation and relevancy mappings. Teams define initial tokenisation schemes, discuss what conditionally helps what, connect the designs to long-context constraints, and synthesise overlaps between KPAs.

## Wednesday: alignment and model-building

Wednesday asks how models can turn multimodal data into trusted scientific insight. The morning focuses on physical alignment, continuous improvement, monitoring, and risk. KPA teams define what alignment means for their use cases and how it could be measured.

The afternoon compares implementation routes: fine-tuning, training from scratch, hybrid stacks, and agentic operation. Teams compare training and inference stacks for each KPA, look for reusable industry tooling, and converge toward a single shared stack.

## Thursday: MVP roadmap and hackathon

Thursday turns the first three days into a roadmap. Participants summarise use cases, unified datasets, and the shared stack, then estimate the development effort needed for an MVP. The group creates a workback plan, turns it into GitHub tickets, assigns groups, and starts hackathon work.

The target for the afternoon is practical momentum: initial code, a working issue backlog, and the beginning of a CI/CD pipeline.

## Friday: funding, governance, and continuity

Friday asks how the collaboration will be funded, governed, and sustained. The morning covers cost, compute, development resources, open-source governance, network growth, and funding opportunities.

KPA teams produce one-page concept documents and invitee lists. The full group then discusses the MVP timeline, funding timeline, reconvening schedule, and remaining skill gaps. The workshop closes with a summary of next steps and optional continued hackathon work.

## Expected outcomes

By the end of the workshop, the collaboration should have:

- a shared understanding of whether to fine-tune existing models or train from scratch;
- a view of relevant state-of-the-art models, inference methods, compute needs, and costs;
- scientific end-user workflows for each KPA;
- dataset specs and tokenisation semantics for each KPA;
- an open GitHub repo with attendees, issues, todos, and initial code;
- a workback plan for post-workshop development.

In the months after the workshop, KPA teams will curate datasets, the implementation team will build data processing, training, inference, and deployment code, and the collaboration will work toward a white paper, MVP, follow-up funding, and alignment with the European Coalition for AI in Fundamental Physics.
