## Image concept

The poster should show joint human-AI discovery: a human scientist and a machine intelligence back-to-back at the centre, both looking outward into a surrounding web of astrophysical data. The pair should feel like partners with complementary roles, not adversaries and not a human using a generic chatbot.

Around them, the web can contain small visual nodes for different kinds of physical evidence: a galaxy image, a spectrum, a time series, a catalogue row, a simulation cube, a gravitational-wave-like curve, an instrument response, a code/provenance record, and a hypothesis/evidence marker. It does not need to be exhaustive. The important point is that many different data products are linked into one scientific field of view.

The final poster should be readable at a glance in the Leiden Observatory elevator lobby: one strong central image, limited text, and a clear icon-like silhouette that still works when reused online or on social media.

### Image material

We have included a generated concept image as a starting point. It is a PNG at 1448 x 1086 pixels, so it meets the requested 1200 x 900 pixel minimum for concept use. The designer may use it directly as reference, redraw it, or source/create a higher-fidelity image in TIFF, JPG, or EPS format.

Useful source or illustration directions:

- a human and machine partner looking outward together from the centre;
- a web or relevancy graph connecting different astrophysical data types;
- small data nodes suggesting image, spectrum, time series, simulation, code/provenance, and hypothesis;
- a restrained astronomical palette with distinct accents for data types and relationships.

Please avoid generic robot heads, glowing brains, binary-code backgrounds, or purely decorative AI imagery. The central idea is not AI in space; it is humans and machines navigating connected physical evidence.

## Lay explanation of the workshop

The consortium has two pillars.

The first is designing how humans and machines jointly study the cosmos. Humans ask the scientific questions, judge evidence, and decide what counts as discovery. AI systems can help retrieve, compare, simulate, and test evidence across archives that are too large for unaided human attention.

The second is building a joint-representation model for astrophysics: a foundation model that learns a common language for images, spectra, time series, catalogues, simulations, instruments, code, and provenance.

The key scientific ambition is to accelerate serendipity. We want systems that help researchers notice rare objects, surprising analogues, missing context, and cross-instrument relationships they might not know to ask for.

## Workshop topics

- Monday: Shared vocabulary, flagship use cases, and evaluation targets for human-AI discovery.
- Tuesday: A relevancy graph linking observations, simulations, instruments, code, provenance, hypotheses, and results.
- Wednesday: Long-context model architecture and training protocol for heterogeneous physical data.
- Thursday: MVP design and hackathon work on schemas, examples, evaluation specs, and prototype code.
- Friday: Consortium roadmap for funding, governance, partnerships, and post-workshop continuity.
